The current working tree was audited against the dedicated ERC-4626 checklist. Severity follows the repository's master definitions: Critical means direct unprivileged theft or permanent loss; High means realistic loss/freeze, a privileged-but-plausible loss trigger, or pool-wide liveness failure; Medium means conditional loss, silent accounting corruption, or a meaningful safety gap; Low means limited/unlikely impact; Info means documentation or hygiene only.

## [ERC4626-1] Conditional virtual values leave the classic donation-inflation attack fully exploitable
**Severity**: Critical
**Category**: evm-audit-erc4626
**Location**: `VaultMath.convertToShares()` / `VaultMath.convertToAssets()` (`src/libraries/VaultMath.sol:17-72`)
**Description**: The purported virtual offset is substituted only when a denominator or numerator is zero: `totalAssets == 0 ? VIRTUAL_ASSETS : totalAssets` and `totalShares == 0 ? VIRTUAL_SHARES : totalShares`. After the first nonzero deposit, neither virtual value participates in conversions. An attacker can therefore mint one share for one USDC base unit, donate assets directly to the vault, force a victim deposit to round to zero shares, then redeem the sole share for the attacker's donation plus the victim's entire deposit. The equal `1e18` constants do not mitigate this because they disappear after the first share exists. This is reachable today with ordinary USDC transfers and public ERC-4626 entrypoints.
**Proof of Concept**: In an empty vault, the attacker deposits `1`, receiving `1` share because both substituted virtual values are `1e18`. The attacker directly transfers `100` USDC base units to the vault, making `totalAssets=101` and `totalSupply=1`. A victim deposits `100`; `previewDeposit` returns `floor(100*1/101)=0`, but `deposit` still transfers all `100` and mints zero shares. With `totalAssets=201`, the attacker redeems the only share and receives all `201`, recovering the donation and stealing the victim's `100`.
**Recommendation**: Use a proven virtual-offset implementation that adds virtual assets and shares on every conversion, such as OpenZeppelin's `assets.mulDiv(totalSupply() + 10 ** _decimalsOffset(), totalAssets() + 1, rounding)`. Choose an offset consistent with six-decimal USDC/share precision. Also reject deposits that would mint zero shares and add an invariant covering first deposit, direct donation, victim deposit, and full redemption.

## [ERC4626-2] Ignored rounding modes allow withdrawals that burn zero shares and overpay redemptions
**Severity**: Critical
**Category**: evm-audit-erc4626
**Location**: `_convertToShares()` / `_convertToAssets()` (`src/NavyVaultSRCLA.sol:691-712`), `VaultMath.convertToShares()` / `VaultMath.convertToAssets()` (`src/libraries/VaultMath.sol:31-72`)
**Description**: Both ERC-4626 conversion overrides discard the requested `Math.Rounding` mode. Asset-to-share conversion always floors, so `previewWithdraw` rounds in the user's favor instead of up. Share-to-asset conversion always ceils, so `convertToAssets`, `previewRedeem`, and `maxWithdraw` round in the user's favor instead of down. Whenever price per share exceeds one, a sufficiently small withdrawal calculates zero shares, transfers nonzero USDC, and leaves the caller's share balance unchanged. Repetition extracts accrued yield/donations. Repeated small redemptions also collect a ceiling remainder per call, transferring value from later cohorts to early redeemers.
**Proof of Concept**: Let `totalSupply=100`, `totalAssets=110`, and an attacker own one share. Incorrect `maxWithdraw(attacker)` returns `ceil(1*110/100)=2`, so `withdraw(1, attacker, attacker)` passes. Incorrect `previewWithdraw(1)` returns `floor(1*100/110)=0`; inherited `_withdraw` burns zero shares and transfers one USDC base unit. The attacker repeats until the ten units of yield are gone while retaining the share. Circle USDC accepts one-base-unit transfers, so six-decimal precision does not stop the loop.
**Recommendation**: Pass the rounding argument through and use full-precision `Math.mulDiv` with Floor for `convertToShares`, `convertToAssets`, `previewDeposit`, and `previewRedeem`, and Ceil for `previewMint` and `previewWithdraw`. Add invariants that a nonzero successful withdrawal always burns nonzero shares, previews do not favor users, and splitting an action cannot improve aggregate output.

## [ERC4626-3] Max functions advertise strategy liquidity that state-changing withdrawals never retrieve
**Severity**: High
**Category**: evm-audit-erc4626
**Location**: `maxWithdraw()` / `maxRedeem()` / `synchronousLiquidity()` (`src/NavyVaultSRCLA.sol:237-245,665-684`)
**Description**: `synchronousLiquidity()` adds adapter `maxWithdrawable()` amounts, and `maxWithdraw`/`maxRedeem` expose that sum as immediately executable. The vault does not override ERC-4626 `_withdraw`, however, so `withdraw` and `redeem` never call `_divest` or otherwise pull assets from strategies; inherited execution only burns shares and transfers current idle USDC. Thus standard calls revert despite remaining within the advertised maximum whenever requested assets exceed idle balance. The fallback makes the overstatement worse: if `maxWithdrawable()` reverts, it assumes the adapter's entire tracked position is liquid. This is a pool-wide withdrawal failure under normal allocation and violates the requirement that max functions bound actual execution.
**Proof of Concept**: With 50 USDC idle and 950 tracked in an adapter that reports 950 withdrawable, `synchronousLiquidity()` and the sole holder's `maxWithdraw` report 1,000. `withdraw(1_000e6, ...)` passes its maximum check, burns shares, then inherited `_withdraw` tries to transfer 1,000 USDC from a 50-USDC balance and reverts. No adapter call is made. If the adapter liquidity view reverts, the catch branch still adds all 950.
**Recommendation**: Before burning shares, deterministically divest enough from known adapters to cover the requested assets, use only `address(this)` as withdrawal recipient, and enforce actual USDC balance deltas and loss limits. If synchronous divestment is not implemented, cap both max functions to idle USDC only. On failed liquidity reads, return/contribute zero rather than assuming full liquidity. Test `preview <= max => state method succeeds` across idle, allocated, illiquid, paused, and failing-adapter states.

## [ERC4626-4] An active adapter can be registered repeatedly and its assets counted multiple times
**Severity**: High
**Category**: evm-audit-erc4626
**Location**: `registerAdapter()` / `totalAssets()` / `synchronousLiquidity()` (`src/NavyVaultSRCLA.sol:210-225,272-295,665-684`)
**Description**: `AdapterState.Active` is enum value zero, the same value returned for an uninitialized mapping entry. The duplicate guard permits any adapter whose state equals zero, so it permits an already-registered active adapter and ignores the dedicated `registeredAdapters` mapping. Each call appends the same address to `_activeAdapters`; both `totalAssets()` and `synchronousLiquidity()` then add the single `strategyAssets[adapter]` value once per duplicate entry. A plausible admin configuration retry can instantly multiply reported NAV without adding assets. Inflated NAV under-mints subsequent depositors and lets earlier cohorts redeem against assets belonging to later cohorts. Removal deletes only one array occurrence, making recovery error-prone.
**Proof of Concept**: Register an adapter holding 100 USDC of strategy value; `_activeAdapters=[adapter]` and `totalAssets` includes 100. Call `registerAdapter(adapter,...)` again while it is Active. The guard does not revert, the address is appended again, and `_syncStrategyAssets` restores the same 100 mapping value. `totalAssets()` now iterates two entries and reports 200 although the adapter still holds only 100. Repeating registration multiplies the phantom NAV and loop cost.
**Recommendation**: Reject registration whenever `registeredAdapters[adapter]` is true, including Active, Disabled, and Impaired states. If re-admission after Removed is required, implement an explicit transition that proves no stale array occurrence remains. Ensure `_activeAdapters` is a set, cap its size, and add invariants that each adapter appears once and the sum counts every position exactly once.

## [ERC4626-5] Stale strategy accounting lets new depositors capture yield earned by earlier cohorts
**Severity**: High
**Category**: evm-audit-erc4626
**Location**: `totalAssets()` / `_syncStrategyAssets()` / `deposit()` / `mint()` (`src/NavyVaultSRCLA.sol:210-225,250-267,634-641`)
**Description**: `totalAssets()` uses cached `strategyAssets` rather than live adapter values. Deposits and mints do not synchronize adapters before pricing shares; synchronization occurs only at registration and after harvest, while `_syncStrategyAssets` silently retains stale values on failure. Accrued Aave/Compound/Moonwell interest is therefore omitted until a later privileged action. A user entering immediately before synchronization receives shares at a stale low price and captures part of the yield earned by existing holders. Conversely, an unrecognized loss over-mints shares and dilutes later cohorts. The design explicitly requires share issuance to close when a required position cannot be refreshed, but the implementation continues at the stale price.
**Proof of Concept**: Incumbents own 100 shares backed by an adapter tracked at 100 USDC, while its live position has accrued to 110. A new user deposits 100 USDC and receives 100 shares because reported NAV is still 100. A subsequent harvest/sync updates strategy value to 110; actual total assets are 210 and the new holder owns half, worth 105. The newcomer captured 5 USDC of yield earned before entry, reducing incumbents from their fair 110 to 105. A caught adapter read failure can extend the arbitrage window indefinitely.
**Recommendation**: Refresh every required strategy and reward value before share issuance, and revert/return zero capacity when any required read cannot be trusted. Alternatively use conservative monotonic accounting that recognizes yield at controlled checkpoints and closes deposits/mints between the economic accrual and checkpoint. Emit sync/loss events and test cohort fairness around interest accrual, impairment, failed reads, deposits, and mints.

## [ERC4626-6] Realized rewards and recognized losses are counted twice after synchronization
**Severity**: High
**Category**: evm-audit-erc4626
**Location**: `totalAssets()` / `_harvestCore()` / `_divest()` / `_syncStrategyAssets()` (`src/NavyVaultSRCLA.sol:210-225,368-420,586-614,634-641`)
**Description**: `recognizedRewards` and `recognizedLosses` are cumulative adjustments with no lifecycle. A successful reward swap transfers USDC into the vault, where the idle balance already enters `totalAssets`, and then adds the same amount to `recognizedRewards`, immediately double-counting realized yield. On a short divestment, the vault subtracts only `received` from cached strategy value and adds the shortfall to `recognizedLosses`; this can temporarily cancel the phantom residual. Once `_syncStrategyAssets` replaces the residual with the adapter's true lower balance, the permanent `recognizedLosses` subtraction applies the same loss again. Neither accumulator is reduced on realization, sync, or withdrawal. NAV can therefore be inflated or depressed depending on operation history rather than actual managed assets, causing systematic cross-cohort transfers and eventual underfunded/reverting redemptions.
**Proof of Concept**: Reward case: actual/reported assets are 100; a swap sends 10 USDC to the vault and returns 10. Idle becomes 110 and `recognizedRewards` becomes 10, so NAV reports 120 although only 110 exists. Loss case: tracked strategy assets are 100; divesting 50 returns 45 and leaves 50 live. The code records strategy assets as 55 and loss as 5, reporting the correct 95 by coincidence. A later sync sets strategy assets to the true 50 but leaves the loss at 5, so NAV falls to 90 although 45 idle plus 50 strategy assets still equal 95.
**Recommendation**: Establish one accounting identity and explicit state transitions. Realized USDC must be represented solely by the measured idle balance; unrealized reward marks must be removed when realized. Update strategy value from before/after live balances, not `received`, and do not separately subtract a loss already reflected in the new position value. If loss/reward counters are retained for telemetry, exclude cumulative historical values from NAV. Add conservation and cohort-fairness invariants across harvest, partial divest, loss, sync, deposit, and redemption.

## [ERC4626-7] Pause handling returns successful no-op mints and disables only the redeem form of exit
**Severity**: Medium
**Category**: evm-audit-erc4626
**Location**: `mint()` / `maxMint()` / `maxRedeem()` (`src/NavyVaultSRCLA.sol:232-267`)
**Description**: While paused, `maxMint` correctly returns zero, but the `mint` override bypasses that maximum, calculates an asset quote, returns success, and neither transfers assets nor mints the requested shares nor emits `Deposit`. This violates the exact-mint and event requirements and can cause integrations to record a successful position that does not exist. Exit behavior is inconsistent in the opposite direction: `maxWithdraw` remains usable, but `maxRedeem` returns zero, causing `redeem` to revert even though the design says emergency pause permits withdrawals and redemptions. Users/integrations that use shares rather than an asset-denominated withdrawal are unnecessarily frozen until an admin unpauses.
**Proof of Concept**: Pause the vault and call `mint(500e6, user)`. The transaction succeeds and returns a nonzero asset number, but the user's asset balance, share balance, vault balance, total supply, and Deposit events remain unchanged. A holder then calls `redeem(1,...)`; inherited ERC-4626 sees `maxRedeem(holder)=0` and reverts, while an equivalent `withdraw` path can remain enabled.
**Recommendation**: Make pause policy consistent: because `maxMint` is zero and the stated design blocks issuance, paused `mint` should revert through the normal max check (or explicitly with `DepositPaused`). Keep both `withdraw` and `redeem` enabled up to conservative executable liquidity during pause. Never return success from an ERC-4626 state method without performing the exact operation and emitting the required event.

## Historical coverage and no-findings (2026-08-12 baseline)

- Reviewed the complete ERC-4626 surface: `asset`, `totalAssets`, conversions, four previews, four max functions, deposit/mint/withdraw/redeem inherited execution, events, third-party allowance behavior, pause behavior, direct donations, empty/bad-debt states, one-unit rounding, adapter liquidity, rewards, losses, and strategy synchronization.
- Outside the paused no-op override, unpaused `deposit` and `mint` use OpenZeppelin execution and therefore pull exactly the requested native USDC amount/mint exactly the requested shares and emit standard events. Inherited `withdraw`/`redeem` support approved third-party operation.
- `convertToAssets` and `convertToShares` do not vary by caller and do not embed fees/slippage. There are no vault fees or cross-chain share burn/mint paths in scope.
- `maxDeposit` and `maxMint` return zero while deposits are disabled and unbounded capacity otherwise. They do not incorrectly depend on the vault's token balance.
- Circle native USDC on the targeted chains is a fixed six-decimal, non-rebasing, non-fee-on-transfer asset without ERC-777 hooks. Exact-transfer and reentrancy concerns for arbitrary underlyings are accepted as out of scope for this deliberately single-asset vault. Circle pause/denylist powers remain an accepted common-mode external freeze risk.
- Nominal virtual shares and assets use equal constants, but ERC4626-1 explains why their conditional application provides no inflation protection. The custom multiplication can revert on intermediate overflow rather than performing 512-bit full-precision division; this is outside practical USDC supply bounds and was not raised separately.
